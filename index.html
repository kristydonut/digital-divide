What is Digital Divide?

Digital divide refers to the division between people who have access and use of digital media and those who do not. It is the gap that was created with the unequal access to modern information and communication technologies (ICTs). The term was first used to describe the gap of accessibility on computers and the internet but it has since expanded to measure the divide to be a multifaceted issue including factors such as affordability, quality, and relevance. It has caused socioeconomic inequalities and affected opportunities in education, work, and daily life.

Real-World Examples

🎓

Educational Gap

The COVID-19 Pandemic highlighted various problems in accessing digital technology due to the closure of schools. It has caused many students to stay at home and do their homework because of the lack of internet or device access. It created a homework gap for students, especially those who belong to lower-income households.

💼

Employment Gap

The hiring process for many job opportunities has been increasingly moved online. A study conducted by Karagiuli et al. (2021) at the University of Zurich found that age, income, and education influence people's proficiency in searching and applying for jobs online. The lack of internet and digital skills further contributed to the difficulties they experienced in engaging with online job hunting.

🩺

Healthcare Gap

Access to medical healthcare is rapidly becoming a digital privilege. Technology- driven solutions ranging from telehealth platforms to wearable devices aims to tailor the medical needs of patients. However, many patients are unable to take advantage of digital healthcare advancements due to limited internet access, lack of digital literacy, and inaccessibility to new technologies.

📍

Rural Connectivity

As rapid digital transformation progresses, residents in remote locations often face minimal internet access. According to a PIDS study (2024), most of the rural population in the Philippines has limited internet connectivity due to inadequate network coverage, which means they cannot involve themselves in digital learning, online employment, and other internet-related activities to their full potential.
