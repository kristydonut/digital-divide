Understanding the Issues

The digital divide does not only exist as a definition — it affects people in real ways. On this page, we explore the struggles, causes, statistics, and impacts that highlight the seriousness of this issue.

According to DICT data as of September 2022, only 1.8 percent of 47,421 public schools nationwide had access to free Wi-Fi, with the quality and speed of connections varying significantly, limiting student’s access to educational materials like books, articles, videos, journals and e-learning modules

At most, women find online work a little more of a challenge in most rural locations due to the lack of access to devices and unreliable electricity. Some still use basic phones instead of smartphones, while others have to travel far just to get a good internet connection. Even when given online jobs, they are often assigned easier tasks and get paid less. This is an indication that women in the rural areas have further challenges in digital opportunities.

The digital divide poses challenges to students in the field of education. In rural settings, most people only use mobile data, have poor connections and lack digital literacy. All 40 students at Kawayan IS said that poor digital access has a strong impact on them, making learning more challenging and restricting their access to the full potential of online learning.

Philippine patients reported that they found it hard to use digital health services due to the poor internet connection. Calls were ineffective, or sometimes disconnected, even in critical conversations with doctors, which made them frustrated. Some other people did not have sufficient mobile load, good cameras, or appropriate devices so that it was not easy to participate in the consultation. These struggles demonstrate that not all people can maximize the use of digital health services.

A lot of families in the Philippines still struggle to get internet at home. According to a PSA survey, 41.7% said internet subscriptions are too expensive, while 33.2% said the cost of equipment like computers or smartphones is also a problem. On top of that, 29.5% of respondents admitted they don’t know how to use the internet, which also keeps them from being connected.
